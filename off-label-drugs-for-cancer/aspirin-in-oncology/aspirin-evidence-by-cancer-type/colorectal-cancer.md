# Colorectal Cancer

Colorectal cancer is the strongest aspirin page in this topic.

This is not just an epidemiology story. This is the clearest setting where aspirin now has **Phase 3 oncology evidence** in a biomarker-defined subgroup.

The most important current signal is in **PIK3CA-mutated** disease.

### Overview

CRC has the deepest aspirin evidence base.

That fits the biology. **COX-2** is commonly upregulated in colorectal cancer. **PIK3CA** alterations are also common, and platelet signalling, inflammation, and immune escape all matter in this disease.

Just as important, colorectal cancer is where aspirin moved beyond observational promise and into prospective trial-level evidence.

### Key human data

* **Phase 3 trial evidence** supports reduced recurrence in **PIK3CA-mutated** colorectal cancer with low-dose aspirin
* long-term observational studies link aspirin use with lower colorectal-cancer incidence
* **Lynch syndrome** data supports a preventive role with sustained use
* tissue studies suggest less nodal spread and higher immune infiltration in aspirin users

### Why this page matters

The key point is simple.

For aspirin in oncology, colorectal cancer is where the evidence becomes genuinely practice-relevant.

The **ALASCCA Phase 3 trial** gives aspirin something most repurposed agents never get: prospective oncology evidence in a molecularly selected patient group. That does not make aspirin standard of care for all colorectal cancer. It does mean this is no longer a soft, speculative signal.

### What they did in the ALASCCA trial

**ALASCCA** stands for _Aspirin for Localized Advanced Stage Colorectal Cancer with Altered PI3K Pathway_.

It is the first **Phase 3** trial to test aspirin as a precision adjuvant therapy in molecularly selected colorectal-cancer patients.\
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40961426/)

#### The setup

* **Design:** Double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled — the gold-standard trial design.\
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40961426/)
* **Where:** Multiple centres across Sweden and Europe, integrated into the Swedish Colorectal Cancer Registry.\
  [ESCP](https://www.escp.eu.com/research/paper-of-the-month/3050-low-dose-aspirin-for-pi3k-altered-localised-colorectal-cancer)
* **Who:** Patients with **stage I–III rectal cancer** or **stage II–III colon cancer** who had already had surgery, and whose tumour carried a somatic mutation in the PI3K pathway.\
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40961426/)
* **Molecular selection:** Patients were split into two groups based on tumour genetics — **Group A** with **PIK3CA** hotspot mutations in exon 9 or 20, and **Group B** with other moderate-to-high impact mutations in **PIK3CA, PIK3R1, or PTEN**.\
  [OncoDaily](https://oncodaily.com/oncolibrary/alascca-trial-for-aspirin-in-pi3k-mut-crc)
* **Treatment:** **160 mg aspirin once daily** versus matched placebo for **3 years** after surgery.\
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40961426/)
* **Primary goal:** Reduce colorectal-cancer recurrence in **Group A** patients.\
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40961426/)

#### What they found

* In **Group A**, aspirin reduced recurrence risk by about **51%** versus placebo. This was statistically significant and met the primary endpoint.\
  [The ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/issues/june-25-2025/use-of-adjuvant-aspirin-reduces-the-risk-of-recurrence-in-pi3k-mutated-colorectal-cancer/)
* **Group B** also showed a strong benefit. That expands the potentially eligible population beyond the classic **PIK3CA hotspot** group alone.\
  [ESCP](https://www.escp.eu.com/research/paper-of-the-month/3050-low-dose-aspirin-for-pi3k-altered-localised-colorectal-cancer)
* Across the full trial cohort, about **40% of resected colorectal cancers** carried some form of PI3K pathway alteration. This is not a niche subgroup.\
  [ESCP](https://www.escp.eu.com/research/paper-of-the-month/3050-low-dose-aspirin-for-pi3k-altered-localised-colorectal-cancer)
* Lead investigator Dr Anna Martling said the findings could change clinical practice for roughly **one-third of patients with non-metastasised colorectal cancer**.\
  [The ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/issues/june-25-2025/use-of-adjuvant-aspirin-reduces-the-risk-of-recurrence-in-pi3k-mutated-colorectal-cancer/)

#### The honest caveat

Severe adverse events were higher in the aspirin arm, **16.8% versus 11.6%**.

This was driven mainly by **GI bleeding** and other **GI toxicity**. Overall survival data are still maturing. The confirmed benefit so far is in recurrence and disease-free survival, not yet overall survival.\
[OncoDaily](https://oncodaily.com/oncolibrary/alascca-trial-for-aspirin-in-pi3k-mut-crc)

#### Why it matters

ALASCCA is the first biomarker-driven randomised trial of adjuvant aspirin in cancer to fully meet its endpoint.

The results were published in the _New England Journal of Medicine_ in 2025 and helped drive **NCCN Guidelines Version 4.2025**, which added formal support for **PIK3CA testing** in stage II–III colon cancer and low-dose aspirin, **100–162 mg daily for 3 years**, in confirmed mutation carriers.\
[The ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/issues/june-25-2025/use-of-adjuvant-aspirin-reduces-the-risk-of-recurrence-in-pi3k-mutated-colorectal-cancer/)

### Clinical positioning

Aspirin is still not standard of care for colorectal cancer as a whole.

However, **PIK3CA-mutated colorectal cancer** is now the clearest setting for a serious adjuvant discussion. **Lynch syndrome** also remains an important evidence-based subgroup.

The right framing is not "aspirin might help somewhere in CRC." The right framing is that aspirin now has **Phase 3 support in a defined colorectal-cancer subgroup**, while broader CRC use still needs more selection and confirmation.
