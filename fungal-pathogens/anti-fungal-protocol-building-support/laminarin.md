# Laminarin

Laminarin is the “turn down the alarm” layer in this protocol.

It is the β-glucan found in Modifilan seaweed extract.

### What laminarin is

Laminarin, or laminaran, is a bioactive polysaccharide extracted from brown seaweed.

### How it works

Laminarin binds the Dectin-1 receptor on immune cells such as macrophages, neutrophils, and dendritic cells.

In this protocol, the key point is that it can occupy Dectin-1 without strongly activating it the way pathogenic fungal β-glucans do.

### The practical idea

Think of it as a “do not disturb” sign on an overactive alarm.

Other antifungal pathways can still work through receptors such as TLR2, Dectin-2, complement, and the mannose receptor.

### What may change downstream

The worksheet links laminarin with:

* lower oxidative stress
* less glutathione drain
* lower ATF4 survival signalling
* better mitochondrial efficiency
* better shift from survival mode toward repair mode

### Why this matters

The goal is not immunosuppression.

The goal is to remove an overstimulating signal that helps pathogens persist while still leaving antifungal defence routes available.

### Research notes from the worksheet

The worksheet highlights:

* mouse infection models showing less runaway inflammation and tissue damage
* colorectal-cancer research where blocking Dectin-1 reduced tumour growth
* immune-support findings involving dendritic-cell maturation, T-cell activity, and NK-cell function

### Dosing and product notes

**Suggested range:** 4–6 capsules daily, roughly 700–1,050 mg laminarin per day.

**Suggested product:** Modifilan seaweed extract.

**Timing:** split doses across the day.

**Cycling:** four weeks on, one week off.

**Special caution:** pause laminarin 48 hours before radioactive iodine scans if needed.

### Safety note

Seaweed products can matter in iodine-sensitive contexts.

Review that with your practitioner if iodine is relevant to your case.
