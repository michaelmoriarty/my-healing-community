# Usnea Tincture

Usnea is the fungal-defence targeting layer in this protocol.

### What usnea is

Usnea, or Old Man’s Beard, is a lichen that contains usnic acid.

This guide positions usnic acid (UA) as a bridge between antifungal and anticancer logic.

For anyone keen to explore the anti-cancer mechanisms, see [Usnic Acid / Usnea in Oncology](../../natural-medicines/usnic-acid-usnea/usnic-acid-usnea-in-oncology-overview.md). That section unpacks the broader oncology evidence around usnic acid.

### How it works in this guide

Usnic acid is presented as targeting 14-3-3 proteins.

These proteins act as master regulators in many cancer cells and are also important for fungal virulence and invasion.

### Why that matters

When 14-3-3 function is disrupted, usnic acid may help:

* reduce fungal virulence
* shorten hyphal growth
* reduce toxin output
* weaken persistence mechanisms
* interfere with aggressive survival signalling in abnormal cells

### Why does it come early in the sequence

Usnea is not presented as the main direct-kill step.

It is framed as a weakening step that makes later antifungal pressure more effective.

### Dosing notes

**Suggested range:** 100–300 mg usnic acid daily.

**Form:** usually a high-quality alcohol-based tincture taken in water.

**Timing:** see the schedule page for sequencing.

### Safety note

Usnic acid can be hepatotoxic at higher doses.

Dark urine or upper abdominal pain warrants prompt review.

Use careful dosing and proper liver monitoring.
