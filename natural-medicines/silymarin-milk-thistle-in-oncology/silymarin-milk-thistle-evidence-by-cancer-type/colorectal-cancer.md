# Colorectal Cancer

## Silymarin in Colorectal Cancer <a href="#silymarin-in-colorectal-cancer" id="silymarin-in-colorectal-cancer"></a>

### Overview <a href="#overview" id="overview"></a>

Silymarin, a polyphenolic flavonoid complex extracted from milk thistle (_Silybum marianum_), has demonstrated significant anticancer activity in colorectal cancer preclinical models. Research indicates its potential to inhibit tumour proliferation, induce apoptosis, modulate key signalling pathways such as Wnt/β-catenin and PI3K/AKT/mTOR, reduce inflammatory markers, enhance immune responses, and synergise with conventional chemotherapy. Studies investigate both standalone effects and interactions with chemotherapeutic agents, positioning silymarin as a promising adjunctive agent worthy of further clinical investigation.

### How Silymarin May Work in Colorectal Cancer <a href="#how-silymarin-may-work-in-colorectal-cancer" id="how-silymarin-may-work-in-colorectal-cancer"></a>

Silymarin exerts its effects through multiple interconnected mechanisms. Key pathways identified in laboratory studies include:

* Suppressing proliferation and inducing apoptosis via modulation of the Bax/Bcl-2 ratio—increasing pro-apoptotic Bax and decreasing anti-apoptotic Bcl-2
* Inhibiting the Wnt/β-catenin pathway by inducing GSK3β-dependent proteasomal degradation of β-catenin and Axin-dependent downregulation of TCF4, thereby reducing nuclear β-catenin levels and transcriptional activity of target genes (e.g., c-Myc, cyclin D1)
* Altering key cancer pathways such as PI3K/AKT/mTOR and NF-κB signalling, which are central to tumour survival, growth, and therapy resistance
* Inducing G1 cell cycle arrest through inhibition of cyclin-dependent kinase activity and upregulation of cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors p21^CIP1^ and p27^KIP1^
* Promoting apoptosis via extrinsic (death receptor) and intrinsic (mitochondrial) pathways, evidenced by increased calreticulin exposure, ATP secretion, HMGB1 secretion (markers of immunogenic cell death), and caspase activation
* Exhibiting cytotoxic and apoptotic potency, particularly when formulated in nanoparticle delivery systems (e.g., tin(IV) oxide, chitosan) to overcome bioavailability limitations
* Inhibiting the secretion of pro-angiogenic factors from tumour cells, thereby reducing vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and interfering with tumour blood supply
* Demonstrating antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties that may protect normal tissues while sensitising tumour cells to oxidative stress
* Suppressing mitogenic and cell survival signalling pathways, including epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2)
* Modulating the tumour immune microenvironment by increasing inflammatory CD8+ T cell responses in tumour-bearing mice
* Targeting cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) by reducing proliferation, colony formation, and migration, and decreasing key fibroblast activation markers such as α-SMA and POSTN

### Findings by Colorectal Cancer Models <a href="#findings-by-colorectal-cancer-models" id="findings-by-colorectal-cancer-models"></a>

Silymarin and its major active component silibinin have demonstrated activity across various colorectal cancer cell lines and murine models:

### Human Colorectal Cancer Cell Lines <a href="#human-colorectal-cancer-cell-lines" id="human-colorectal-cancer-cell-lines"></a>

* In CT-26 (murine colon carcinoma) and Caco-2 (human epithelial colorectal adenocarcinoma) cells, silymarin significantly reduced cell viability and proliferation in a dose-dependent manner, with IC₅₀ values ranging from approximately 2.3 to 85.2 mg/mL depending on the assay and cell line
* Silymarin treatment increased markers of immunogenic cell death (calreticulin exposure, ATP secretion, HMGB1 secretion) in both CT-26 and Caco-2 cells
* In SW480 cells (harbouring mutant APC gene), silibinin significantly decreased nuclear and cytoplasmic levels of beta-catenin, downregulated beta-catenin target genes (c-Myc, cyclin D1), inhibited CDK8 expression, and suppressed TCF-4 transcriptional activity; no significant effects were observed in HCT116 cells (wildtype APC), indicating selective action on beta-catenin pathway in mutant APC backgrounds
* In HT-29, LoVo, and SW480 cells, silibinin decreased colonosphere formation (a stem cell characteristic) and reduced mRNA/protein levels of cancer stem cell regulatory molecules (LGR5, ASCL2, CD44, CD133, OCT4, NANOG, MSI-1, BMI-1, HES-1)
* In CaCo-2 cells, silymarin-loaded tin(IV) nanoparticles exhibited enhanced bioavailability and antiproliferative effects, reducing cell viability over 6–24 hours of treatment
* Silymarin has been shown to downregulate gene products involved in tumour cell proliferation (EGFR, COX-2) and invasion (matrix metalloproteinases)

### Murine Models of Colorectal Cancer <a href="#murine-models-of-colorectal-cancer" id="murine-models-of-colorectal-cancer"></a>

* In CT-26 tumour-bearing mice, silymarin treatment decreased tumour size and increased survival of colorectal cancer cells compared to controls, with effects comparable to doxorubicin in some studies
* Silymarin reduced the expression of genes related to cell proliferation (β-catenin, cyclin D1) and anti-apoptotic factors (Bcl-2) while increasing pro-apoptotic Bax in tumour tissues
* The number of CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) increased in the spleen of silymarin-treated mice compared to untreated mice, indicating enhanced anti-tumour immune response
* Histological analysis revealed decreased tumour size and improved survival in silymarin-treated tumour-bearing mice
* In an AOM/DSS-induced colorectal cancer model, silibinin effectively reduced POSTN and α-SMA protein levels in cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and inhibited CAF proliferation, colony formation, and migration
* Silymarin supplementation significantly mitigated elevated inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6), liver function enzyme levels, and hyperlipidemia in carcinogen-induced models
* Treatment regimens significantly reduced serum LDH levels and enhanced antioxidant activities (SOD, GPx) compared to carcinogen-exposed controls
* Silymarin ameliorated carcinogen-induced polyp multiplicity, adenoma formation, dysplastic changes, and lymphocytic aggregation in preclinical models

### Cancer Stem Cell Targeting <a href="#cancer-stem-cell-targeting" id="cancer-stem-cell-targeting"></a>

Cancer stem cells are a subpopulation of cells believed to drive recurrence and treatment resistance in colorectal cancer. They are identified by surface markers including CD44^+^EpCAM^high^ or ALDH^+^ expression.

Laboratory and in vivo studies have shown that silymarin, particularly silibinin, targets colorectal cancer stem cells:

* Silibinin strongly decreases the percentage of colonosphere formation (a stem cell characteristic) in CRC cell lines (HT-29, LoVo, SW480)
* This effect on cancer stem cells is mediated via blocking of interleukin (IL)-4/6 signalling, leading to decreased IL-4/6 induced activation of STAT-3 and NF-κB transcriptional activity
* Silibinin significantly reduces mRNA and protein levels of cancer stem cell regulatory molecules and markers (LGR5, ASCL2, CD44, CD133, OCT4, NANOG, MSI-1, BMI-1, HES-1)
* Symmetric self-renewal of cancer stem cells is decreased, while asymmetric self-renewal is increased, shifting cell division towards symmetric proliferation and generating more differentiated clones
* Silibinin significantly reduces the booster signals of macrophages towards cancer stem cells, resulting in decreased colonosphere numbers under both normoxic and hypoxic conditions
* In vivo studies show that silibinin treatment reduces the tumorigenic potential of cancer stem cells (CD44^+^EpCAM^high^ CRC cells) and inhibits cancer stem cell self-renewal in serial transplantation studies
* After treatment, silibinin inhibits both cancer stem cell and bulk daughter cell proliferation, and transforms/differentiates the CD44^+^ population into a CD44^- phenotype

### Chemosensitisation: Doxorubicin and Other Combinations <a href="#chemosensitisation-doxorubicin-and-other-combinati" id="chemosensitisation-doxorubicin-and-other-combinati"></a>

Silymarin demonstrates notable synergy with doxorubicin and other chemotherapeutic agents:

* Silymarin suppresses proliferation and PD-L1 expression in colorectal cancer cells almost as much as doxorubicin, suggesting comparable anti-proliferative efficacy
* In combination with doxorubicin, silymarin enhances apoptotic effects while reducing oxidative stress in normal hepatocytes
* Silymarin sensitises tumours to chemotherapeutic agents through down-regulation of the multidrug resistance protein (MDR1/P-glycoprotein) and other mechanisms
* Dietary silymarin supplementation with chemotherapeutic agents significantly mitigated elevated inflammatory cytokines, liver function enzyme levels, and hyperlipidemia
* Significant upregulation of BAX and downregulation of Bcl-2 were observed in combination treatments, enhancing pro-apoptotic signalling
* Treatment regimens ameliorated carcinogen-induced polyp multiplicity, adenoma formation, dysplastic changes, and lymphocytic aggregation when combined with chemotherapy
* Silymarin alone and in combination with irinotecan (SMN-IRI) produced a greater reduction in TNF-α levels than capecitabine-based regimens (SMN-CAP), while effects on CRP levels varied by regimen
* Silymarin enhances chemotherapy efficacy and reduces carcinogen- and chemotherapy-induced hepatotoxicity through antioxidant and anti-inflammatory actions
* Selenium combined with silybin has been shown to enhance therapeutic effects in colorectal cancer models

### Radiotherapy Support <a href="#radiotherapy-support" id="radiotherapy-support"></a>

Direct radiotherapy studies for silymarin in colorectal cancer are limited, but the mechanistic rationale supports investigation:

* Silymarin’s antioxidant capacity may protect normal tissue from radiation-induced oxidative damage
* By inhibiting NF-κB and reducing inflammatory cytokines, silymarin could mitigate radiotherapy-induced inflammation
* Its anti-angiogenic effects may interfere with tumour repair following radiation-induced damage
* Silymarin’s immunomodulatory effects may enhance antitumor immune responses following radiation-induced antigen release
* Preclinical models in other cancers show silymarin reduces radiation-induced fibrosis and tissue damage via TGF-β/Smad pathway inhibition
* Clinical trials evaluating topical or oral silymarin during colorectal radiotherapy are warranted given its safety profile and mechanistic plausibility

### Practical Interpretation for Patients <a href="#practical-interpretation-for-patients" id="practical-interpretation-for-patients"></a>

Silymarin is not a treatment for colorectal cancer, but research suggests it may offer supportive benefits as an adjunct:

* It may suppress tumour proliferation and induce apoptosis through modulation of Bax/Bcl-2, caspase cascades, and immunogenic cell death markers
* It shows potential to inhibit the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, a key driver in colorectal carcinogenesis, particularly in mutant APC contexts
* It appears to target cancer stem cells, which are implicated in recurrence and therapy resistance
* It may enhance chemotherapy efficacy (particularly with doxorubicin) while reducing hepatotoxicity and other organ toxicities
* It has demonstrated favourable safety profiles in preclinical and clinical studies, with no significant liver or kidney toxicity observed at therapeutic doses
* Any consideration should involve discussion with oncology professionals regarding timing (e.g., avoiding high-dose antioxidants during radiotherapy if pro-oxidant tumour effects are desired) and formulation (standardised extracts vs. nanoparticles)

### PD-L1 suppression and immune activation

A notable 2024 finding showed that silymarin suppressed both proliferation and PD-L1 expression in colorectal cancer cells while increasing inflammatory CD8+ T-cell populations in tumour-bearing mice (good news). This gives Milk Thistle / Silymarin real immuno-oncology interest in this cancer type.
