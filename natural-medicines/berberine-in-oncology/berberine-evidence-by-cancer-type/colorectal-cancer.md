# Colorectal Cancer

Colorectal cancer is where berberine's evidence base is strongest and most clinically actionable.

Oral berberine concentrates in gastrointestinal tissue after dosing. That means the gut and colorectal mucosa see far higher exposure than systemic circulation.

That gives colorectal prevention and adjunctive use a real pharmacokinetic advantage.

### Why berberine stands out here

Colorectal cancer is where berberine has its clearest bridge between mechanism and human data.

It combines:

* gastrointestinal tissue exposure advantage
* microbiome relevance
* direct glycolysis suppression
* apoptosis signalling
* early resistance-reversal work
* one meaningful human randomised trial in the adenoma-prevention setting

The microbiome point matters more here than in most other tumour types.

Gut microbial ecology has a direct role in colorectal tumorigenesis, inflammation, and recurrence risk.

### Human clinical evidence

Berberine's most important oncology-related human data comes from the multicentre, double-blind, randomised trial published in _The Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology_.

Reported features include:

* **891 patients** after removal of 1 to 6 colorectal adenomas
* **berberine 300 mg twice daily** for **2 years**
* adenoma recurrence of **36%** with berberine versus **47%** with placebo
* relative risk **0.77**, roughly a **23% recurrence reduction**
* lower occurrence of advanced adenoma lesions
* no serious adverse events over **2 years**
* constipation reported in about **1%** of the berberine group

This does **not** prove berberine treats colorectal cancer.

It does make berberine one of the few natural compounds with real human colorectal chemoprevention data.

### Main preclinical mechanisms in colorectal cancer

#### Glycolysis disruption

Berberine suppresses glycolysis-linked targets including **HK2** and **LDHA**.

That reduces glucose consumption and lactate output, placing metabolic pressure on colorectal tumour cells.

#### Apoptosis via the CASC2 / Bcl-2 axis

Berberine has been linked to activation of the **CASC2 / Bcl-2** axis, shifting colorectal cells back toward apoptosis.

#### Cetuximab sensitisation

One of the most interesting CRC findings is combination work with **cetuximab**.

Preclinical studies suggest berberine may help overcome resistance in **KRAS-mutant** colorectal models.

That is clinically relevant because this remains a difficult treatment setting.

#### Microbiome remodelling

Berberine also has a microbiome angle that is unusually relevant in colorectal disease.

This matters because gut ecology, inflammation, and tumour-promoting microbial patterns are directly tied to colorectal carcinogenesis.

It also helps explain why oral berberine is such a natural fit for this cancer type.

### Clinical integration notes

* **Post-polypectomy:** this is the clearest berberine discussion point to raise with a gastroenterologist.
* **KRAS-mutant CRC:** cetuximab-sensitisation data is still preclinical, but it is unusually relevant.
* **5-FU or capecitabine:** low-dose berberine has attenuated some cytotoxic effects in selected gastric and colorectal models, so treatment timing deserves discussion with the oncology team.
* **GI tolerance:** cramping, loose stools, constipation, or discomfort can happen. Start low and titrate.

### Practical interpretation

This is berberine's strongest cancer-type page.

For readers focused on prevention after adenoma removal, colorectal disease offers the most clinically grounded reason to care about berberine.

For active colorectal cancer, the evidence is still mainly preclinical.

Even so, the combination of gut exposure, metabolic pressure, microbiome effects, and cetuximab-sensitisation research makes this one of the most compelling tumour settings in the whole berberine literature.
