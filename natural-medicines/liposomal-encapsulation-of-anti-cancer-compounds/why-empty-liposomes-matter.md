# Why Empty Liposomes Matter

Empty liposomes are usually treated as neutral carriers that will greatly increase bioavailability of drugs and compounds.

_e.g When researching Fiestin we learnt that from a 150 mg liposomal capsule, you can realistically expect somewhere between **90-140 mg actually absorbed** (60-95% bioavailability), compared to maybe **3-10 mg** from the same amount of regular non-liposomal fisetin in the same conditions_

A 2021 paper by A.I. Soloviev argues that some **empty phosphatidylcholine liposomes** may also have biological activity of their own.

{% hint style="warning" %}
This section is educational only. It is not medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. The biological relevance discussed here is still emerging and should not be treated as settled clinical fact.
{% endhint %}

### Simple summary

Liposomes are tiny bubble-like structures made from lipids similar to cell membranes.

They are usually discussed as vehicles that improve delivery.

The Soloviev paper argues that some empty liposomes are **not fully inert**.

In that paper, phosphatidylcholine liposomes appeared able to:

* influence cell-membrane behaviour
* help restore stressed blood-vessel function
* affect specific membrane channels
* change the local membrane environment after merging into cell membranes

The proposed mechanism is not that the empty liposome carries a classic drug payload.

It is that the liposome itself may alter membrane behaviour once it integrates into the membrane surface.
