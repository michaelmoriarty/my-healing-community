# Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

NSCLC is an early but increasingly interesting setting for WFA.

The strongest reasons are EMT suppression, paclitaxel synergy, and immune checkpoint sensitisation.

### Cell data

WFA suppresses `TGFβ1` and `TNFα`-driven EMT in NSCLC cell lines such as `A549` and `H1299`.

At sub-cytotoxic concentrations, it reduces adhesion, migration, and invasion without needing overt cell killing. That makes the anti-metastatic signal especially interesting.

WFA has also shown synergy with paclitaxel in NSCLC models, improving effects on proliferation and invasive behaviour.

### Animal data

The most important animal data comes from syngeneic lung cancer work. In that setting, WFA combined with anti-PD-L1 therapy reduced tumour growth more effectively than either agent alone and increased `CD8+` T-cell infiltration.

This makes NSCLC the strongest current disease setting for WFA's checkpoint-sensitisation hypothesis.

### Human data

There is no NSCLC-specific human efficacy trial yet.

### Clinical setting assessment

NSCLC remains early, but the translational logic is strong.

Because checkpoint inhibitors are already part of standard care in many lung cancer settings, WFA fits a clinically recognisable design space. That makes this disease area more interesting than its current evidence volume alone might suggest.

### Bottom line

NSCLC is not yet one of the deepest WFA evidence bases, but it may be one of the most strategically important for future combination trials.
