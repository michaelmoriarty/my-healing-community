# Sourcing Quality Shikonin

{% hint style="warning" %}
Shikonin is a category where raw ingredient quality, standardisation, and formulation matter more than label confidence.
{% endhint %}

Shikonin is usually derived from _Lithospermum erythrorhizon_ root bark.

The source plant matters.

The extraction method matters even more.

### What matters before price

1. Clear standardisation to **shikonin**, not only total naphthoquinones
2. Third-party identity and contaminant testing
3. Transparent plant origin and extraction method
4. A delivery system that addresses solubility, if the goal is meaningful exposure
5. Stability testing where possible, because quinone compounds can degrade

### Why standardisation matters

Root powders and loose herbal extracts can vary widely.

That makes “traditional-use” sourcing a poor guide if the real goal is repeatable exposure.

Shikonin is not a category where vague herbal equivalence is enough.
