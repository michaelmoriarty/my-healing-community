# Shikonin Evidence by Cancer Type

This section groups the shikonin evidence into cancer-specific pages.

That matters because shikonin is not equally relevant in every tumour type.

Its strongest mechanisms depend on context.

PKM2 dependence, estrogen signalling, EMT biology, and resistance patterns differ between cancers.

### Available pages

* [**Breast Cancer**](breast-cancer.md) — strongest current case, with ER-pathway disruption, PKM2 relevance, necroptosis, and tamoxifen synergy
* [**Bladder Cancer**](bladder-cancer.md) — most complete resistance-reversal story, centred on PKM2 and cisplatin resistance
* [**Oesophageal Cancer**](oesophageal-cancer.md) — one of the better-supported non-breast settings, with PKM2 and STAT3-linked glycolysis suppression plus in vivo data
* [**Ovarian Cancer**](ovarian-cancer.md) — microenvironment-focused evidence, especially exosome and macrophage biology
* [**Cervical Cancer**](cervical-cancer.md) — early but specific work on EMT suppression and PI3K/AKT/mTOR-related signalling
* [**Lung Cancer**](lung-cancer.md) — PI3K/AKT/mTOR relevance and derivative-based gefitinib sensitisation findings
* [**Thyroid Cancer**](thyroid-cancer.md) — emerging ferroptosis signal in anaplastic thyroid cancer

If a cancer type is not listed here yet, it can be added later as the literature grows.

### Why the structure matters

A finding in one cancer type does not automatically transfer to another.

ERα degradation matters far more in luminal breast cancer than in bladder cancer.

Ferroptosis may matter in anaplastic thyroid cancer without being central elsewhere.

That is why shikonin needs a cancer-by-cancer structure rather than one merged summary.
