# Sourcing Quality Apigenin

## Sourcing Quality Apigenin

### What makes a product legitimate

A credible apigenin product should clearly state:

* the amount of apigenin per dose in milligrams
* whether that content is standardised
* the source of the compound
* third-party testing with a batch-specific certificate of analysis

### The biggest buying mistake

The most common mistake is buying a product labelled only as chamomile extract, parsley extract, or a flavonoid complex without stating apigenin content in milligrams.

Those plants may contain apigenin, but the actual amount varies widely with growing conditions, extraction method, and processing. Without a standardised apigenin percentage and a stated milligram dose, the true intake is unknown.

A second mistake is assuming that “high flavonoid” or “high polyphenol” means high apigenin. It does not.

### Direct compound vs extract

* **Direct apigenin:** clearer dosing, easier standardisation, and closer to the form studied in the literature
* **Plant extract standardised to apigenin:** legitimate when the percentage and the actual milligram amount are clearly stated for apigenin itself
* **Unstandardised herbal extracts:** not appropriate for dose-specific integrative oncology use

### Formulation quality

* **Liposomal products:** look for technical evidence of particle size, encapsulation efficiency, and stability
* **Phospholipid complexes:** some preclinical work suggests better bioavailability than standard powder
* **Standard capsule or powder:** cheaper and widely available, but less reliable when trying to approach preclinical exposure logic

For broader formulation context, see [Liposomal Encapsulation of Anti-cancer Compounds](../liposomal-encapsulation-of-anti-cancer-compounds/).

### Purity and testing

Third-party certificates of analysis should confirm:

* identity
* potency in actual milligrams per dose
* absence of contaminants such as heavy metals, microbiological contamination, and extraction solvents

Proprietary blends that do not disclose individual ingredient amounts are not appropriate for dose-specific use.

### Practical summary

* Buy products that specify apigenin in milligrams per dose.
* Confirm third-party testing with a current certificate of analysis.
* Prefer enhanced-delivery formulations when bioavailability is the main concern, but verify the formulation claim.
