# Sourcing Quality Boswellia

For oncology-oriented use, generic Boswellia or frankincense products without meaningful **AKBA standardisation** are usually not enough.

### What to look for

1. a product that states **AKBA content** clearly
2. total boswellic acids are clearly stated
3. no unnecessary fillers
4. confidence about the extract quality and testing
5. practical ability to reach research-relevant daily intake

### Why AKBA matters

Many Boswellia products advertise only total boswellic acids, but the strongest oncology literature focuses specifically on **AKBA**.

That means a product can look strong on paper but still be weak for oncology-oriented use if it does not provide meaningful AKBA.

### Practical buying logic

When comparing Boswellia products, ask:

* How much AKBA does this provide?
* How much total extract is required to reach useful levels?
* Does the product make it practical to dose throughout the day?
* Is the extract likely to be better absorbed with food?

### Practical takeaway

For oncology-oriented discussion, AKBA-standardised Boswellia is more relevant than a generic frankincense or resin-support product.

### Why the piperlongumine difference matters

Piperlongumine may improve the absorption of boswellic acids, which could be beneficial. But during active treatment, some readers may reasonably prefer a formula without it if there are concerns that extra absorption-enhancing effects could also alter concurrent drug and supplement tolerance or side-effect burden.

That does **not** mean piperlongumine is inappropriate. It means the choice between these two formulas may depend in part on how simple and conservative a patient wants the treatment-support stack to be during active therapy.

### Practical buying logic

The main questions are not just price or capsule count. They are:

1. How much **actual AKBA** does the product provide?
2. Is the formula designed to improve absorption?
3. Does it contain anything else, such as piperlongumine, that may change the clinical discussion?
4. Is the product realistic to dose at research-oriented levels without excessive capsule burden?
