# Open Clinical Trial — COAST in Advanced Prostate Cancer

COAST is one of the clearest autophagy-focused clinical trials now recruiting.

It studies a fully oral combination of older generic drugs.

The goal is to block cancer stress-survival pathways from several angles at once.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Trial ID:** NCT05036226

**Official title:** COAST Therapy in Advanced Solid Tumors and Prostate Cancer

**Current setting:** single-centre study at Hollings Cancer Center, Medical University of South Carolina, Charleston, USA
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="success" %}
This trial has only one recruiting site.

That sharply limits access on a world stage.

It still matters far beyond Charleston.

It shows that autophagy-targeted combination strategy is moving from theory into formal human testing.

For the wider community, that is the real signal of hope.
{% endhint %}

### Why this trial matters

Most autophagy discussions stay preclinical.

COAST moves that logic into a formal phase I/II protocol.

It tests a five-drug combination built from familiar oral drugs:

* hydroxychloroquine
* metformin
* sirolimus
* nelfinavir
* dasatinib

What makes the trial notable is not novelty.

It is the deliberate combination design.

Each drug presses on autophagy, mTOR, metabolic stress, or growth signalling.

Together, they aim to leave fewer escape routes.

{% hint style="warning" %}
This is still an investigational regimen.

These are real prescription drugs with real interaction and toxicity risks.

Trial doses should not be copied outside supervised care.
{% endhint %}

### What COAST is trying to do

COAST stands for **Combination of Autophagy Selective Therapeutics**.

The study starts as a dose-finding trial in advanced solid tumours.

It then moves into a prostate-cancer expansion.

The central question is simple.

Can a multi-drug oral autophagy strategy be given safely enough to justify a larger signal-seeking study?

### Who can join

Phase I is broader.

It includes adults with advanced solid tumours after standard systemic options have been exhausted.

Phase II is narrower.

It focuses on advanced or relapsed prostate cancer with PSA at or above 0.1 ng/mL.

Other core requirements include:

* measurable or evaluable disease
* ECOG performance status 0–2
* adequate kidney, liver, and marrow function
* progression after at least one standard systemic regimen

Because this is a single-centre study, travel matters.

That is a practical filter even when eligibility looks strong.

### How the trial is built

The protocol uses a standard 3+3 dose-escalation design.

It starts with a three-drug oral backbone.

It then adds one or two more drugs only if safety remains acceptable.

#### Base combination

The starting backbone is:

* hydroxychloroquine 600 mg twice daily
* metformin 500 mg daily for 7 days, then 1000 mg daily
* sirolimus 0.5 mg daily

#### Added layers

If the base is tolerated, the study tests:

* base plus dasatinib 20 mg daily
* base plus nelfinavir 1250 mg twice daily
* five-drug combinations if earlier levels remain acceptable

The protocol can also escalate nelfinavir further in later dose levels.

The study defines a maximum tolerated mixture.

It then selects a recommended phase II dose.

That dose does not have to be the absolute highest dose tested.

It can be one step lower if tolerability looks better there.

### What the prostate-cancer phase is testing

Once the recommended dose is chosen, the study turns to advanced prostate cancer.

The main endpoint is practical.

It asks how many patients remain free of PSA progression at 16 weeks under PCWG3 criteria.

The phase II part uses a Simon two-stage design.

That means it can stop early if the signal looks too weak.

The decision rules are:

* if none of the first 10 patients are PSA progression-free at 16 weeks, the trial stops
* if at least 1 of 10 meets that mark, the study expands to 29 patients
* if 4 or more of 29 are PSA progression-free at 16 weeks, the regimen is considered promising

This design is small.

It is still useful.

It gives a disciplined first read on whether the approach is worth pursuing.

### Why these drugs were chosen

Each drug hits a different stress-survival node.

#### Hydroxychloroquine

Hydroxychloroquine blocks late-stage autophagy.

It interferes with lysosomal function and autophagic flux.

#### Metformin

Metformin adds metabolic stress.

It activates AMPK and can reduce mTOR signalling.

#### Sirolimus

Sirolimus directly inhibits mTOR.

That increases growth pressure and may deepen dependence on autophagy escape.

#### Nelfinavir

Nelfinavir adds proteotoxic and endoplasmic-reticulum stress.

It also appears to increase autophagy signalling in stressed cells.

#### Dasatinib

Dasatinib inhibits SRC, ABL, and related kinases.

That broadens pressure on tumour survival and adaptive signalling.

### Why this trial stands out in the autophagy section

COAST is not just another single-agent hydroxychloroquine study.

It is one of the most explicit attempts to build a **multi-layer autophagy and stress-pathway strategy** inside a formal oncology trial.

That makes it relevant far beyond prostate cancer.

It gives patients and clinicians a real protocol to watch.

It also gives the wider repurposed-drug field something it often lacks.

It gives a structured way to test a generic-drug combination rather than debating it only in theory.

It is also a reminder of where the field still stands.

Global access is very limited.

The trial footprint is small.

But the fact that this study exists at all is meaningful.

It shows that autophagy is being taken seriously enough to justify a real, protocol-driven human study.

### Practical takeaways

* This is an active recruiting trial for advanced solid tumours and relapsed prostate cancer.
* The study is especially relevant for people interested in autophagy-targeted repurposed-drug strategies.
* It is also relevant for clinicians watching whether generic oral combinations can produce a credible signal in prostate cancer.
* Access is currently limited to one site, but the trial still marks an important step forward for autophagy research in real patients.
* The most useful share term is the official identifier: NCT05036226.
