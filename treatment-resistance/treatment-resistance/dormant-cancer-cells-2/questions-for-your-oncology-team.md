# Questions for Your Oncology Team

Dormancy science is most useful when it sharpens the conversation.

These questions help move the appointment beyond a simple **treat or do not treat** frame.

### Strategy

* Is the plan trying to kill this lesion, keep it dormant, or wake it and then kill it?
* If chemotherapy or local treatment is proposed, which dormancy strategy does it fit?
* Are we aiming for eradication, durable control, or monitoring with a clear threshold to act?

### Mechanisms

* Has anyone looked at autophagy, mTOR, ERK/p38, angiogenesis, or stress-response pathways in my case?
* Are there combinations that target dormant-cell survival rather than only fast-dividing cells?
* If a lesion is small and stable, what features would make you think it is biologically active rather than dormant?

### Inflammation and stress

* What is the plan to reduce peri-treatment inflammation and stress?
* If a procedure is planned, how do we lower avoidable inflammatory and adrenergic stress signals around it?
* Is it reasonable to discuss beta-blockade or other adjunct strategies in my situation?

### Monitoring thresholds

* At what size, growth rate, scan change, biomarker change, or symptom level would you switch from watch and maintain to active intervention?
* How will we monitor for signs that dormant disease may be waking up?
* Which changes would count as meaningful progression rather than noise?

### Why these questions help

These questions do not fight the team.

They clarify assumptions.

That matters because different clinicians may be using very different mental models for the same scan finding.

### Bottom line

A good dormancy question does not ask only **How do we remove this?**

It also asks **What biology are we trying to control, and how will we know when the strategy should change?**
