# Dormancy Glossary

### Dormant cancer cell

A cancer cell that is alive but not actively dividing.

It often survives treatments that mainly target fast-dividing cells.

### Slow-cycling cell

A cell that divides very infrequently.

It overlaps with dormancy but may still divide from time to time.

### Disseminated tumour cell

A cancer cell that has left the original tumour and settled in a distant site.

It may stay clinically silent for a long time.

### Microenvironment

The local neighbourhood around a cancer cell.

This includes blood vessels, immune cells, connective tissue, signalling molecules, and matrix structure.

### ERK/p38 balance

A simplified way to describe one internal signalling balance linked to dormancy.

Higher **p38** tends to favour dormancy.

Higher **ERK** tends to favour proliferation.

### G0/G1 phase

The resting or early part of the cell cycle.

Dormant cells usually pause here instead of moving into active division.

### Autophagy

A cell-recycling process.

Dormant cancer cells often use it to survive under stress.

### Angiogenesis

The growth of new blood vessels.

A more vascular niche can help a quiet focus start growing.

### Dormancy escape

The process by which a dormant or slow-cycling cell starts dividing again.

This is also called reactivation. Reactivation can occur in what appear to be new sites — on post-treatment PET/CT scan reports, in which the targeted lesion is successfully treated but new lesions seem to have popped up.

### Perioperative period

The time before, during, and shortly after surgery.

It is a biologically active window marked by stress, inflammation, and immune change.

### Myeloid-derived suppressor cells

Immune cells that can suppress anti-tumour immunity and support tumour progression.

They often expand in chronic inflammation and stress states.

### NETs

Short for neutrophil extracellular traps.

These are web-like inflammatory structures released by neutrophils that can sometimes support metastasis-related behaviour.

### Senolytic

A drug or compound designed to help clear senescent cells.

Some discussions overlap with dormancy, but senescence and dormancy are not the same state.

### Beta-blocker

A medicine that blocks adrenaline-like signals.

Propranolol is the best-known example discussed in perioperative and adjunct oncology settings.
